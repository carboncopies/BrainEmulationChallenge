# Preregistration, gaba_vm: does late GABA silence reverberation and discard a too-early second pattern in an E4 model? (written 2026-11-22, before any simulation; not edited after launch)
Context: Randal's reply (thread 1a1ce43946326354) says late GABA is fine. In his view it should silence the network AFTER a pattern fires, to stop reverberation and space successive patterns (theta later). He offered to inspect Vm traces and named these knobs: AHP, ADP, max AMPA, NMDA, lateral inhibition. Earlier work: ../../gaba_check_l (GABA is loaded and acts but arrives about 2 ms after the non-cue members fire), ../../headline_i (E4 recall, dyn2.py), ../../gaba_patch (model-file GABA patch).
Constraints: separate patched build only (NES 8011 / API 8010), at most 1 simulation process at a time, NES pinned to 4 cores (taskset); main NES 3156209 / API 1972355 untouched; no NES code change; no git; no email. Model files are only ever edited as temporary copies, which are deleted after use.

## Model and protocol (fixed)
- Network: E4 model V1n20 seed 33333 (headline_i; 96 pyramidal + 20 interneurons; passes all 7 bars), model `v2-hdi-V1n20_s33333_p0d0175_i0d030000`. Memories: design D42 (3 disjoint 6-cell memories, 4 cue + 2 non-cue cells each: G0 {10,11,29,34,63,78} non-cue 29,63; G1 {9,47,50,79,84,86} non-cue 47,50; G2 {1,4,5,35,68,82} non-cue 4,35).
- Programming: R3 recipe (dyn2.pair_list: cue->non-cue T/4 = 4.125 nS per pair, loops capped at 8 nS per cell, Pyr->Int weight 1 with cue->interneuron capped at 10 nS, background Pyr->Pyr min(g, c)). Abstracted functional LIFC, precise spike times, STDP off, Dt 0.1 ms.
- Programming goes into the model file (copy), not through SetPrePostStrength: the API call sets AMPA and zeroes NMDA and GABA on that pair (LIFCNeuron.cpp:543-559), so NMDA cannot be kept with the API. File programming sets each pair's AMPA receptor weights to (target conductance / pair g_peak_sum), and NMDA weights to r x target / NMDA g_peak_sum (r = 0 unless the NMDA knob is on, matching what the API does). GABA weight is 0.2 x factor (0 = silenced). Validation V0: file-programmed vs API-programmed (dyn2 path) at c 1.5, GABA x1, must give identical spike times (expected exactly identical; a difference over 0.01 ms in any spike means the file route is wrong and the knob runs are not used).
- Events per simulation (cue = forced spikes of the 4 cue cells at 0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5 ms; events 500 ms apart, first at 20 ms): e0 G0 alone; e1 G0 then G1 at delta 10 ms (Vm demo); e2 G1 alone; e3 G2 alone; then all 6 ordered pairs (first, second) of G0/G1/G2 x delta 5, 10, 20, 50 ms (24 events). Vm recorded for all 116 cells for the first 1,100 ms (e0, e1); spike times for the whole run.

## Conditions (one simulation each; 37 in total, run one at a time)
- GABA strength x c: GABA silenced (SG), x1 (build: weight 0.2, 0.16 nS per synapse), x2, x5, at c = 0, 1.5 and 3.0 nS (12).
- Knobs at c = 1.5, each with SG, x1 and x5 (24): AHP x0 and AHP x2 (principal fast and slow AHP peak and max conductance), ADP x0 and ADP x3 (principal ADP peak conductance 0.3 nS), NMDA r = 0.56 (the build's own NMDA/AMPA per-synapse ratio 0.55/0.98) and r = 1.0, max AMPA x1.25 (every programmed Pyr->Pyr AMPA conductance), lateral inhibition (cue->interneuron cap removed and Pyr->Int AMPA peak conductance x2).
- V0 API-programmed control (1).
- Bio status, stated before running. Natural Int->Pyr pair at x1 in this model: mean 0.70 nS, median 0.48, p90 1.60, max 5.12, with 1.9% of pairs above 3 nS (Ecker PV->PC mean unitary 0.65 nS, as used in gaba_patch). So x1 is at the literature mean, x2 (mean 1.39, 11% of pairs above 3 nS) is above it, and x5 (mean 3.48, 41% above 3 nS) is outside the natural range: diagnostic only, not a recommendation. bio_ranges.json has no entry for receptor strength, AHP, ADP or NMDA, so for those knobs only the stated factor is reported.

## Measures (defined now)
- Completion: all non-cue members of the cued memory spike within 10 ms of its cue onset.
- Discard of the second cue: in a two-cue event the second memory does not complete, while the same memory completes when cued alone in the same simulation (e2/e3/e0). Also reported: non-cue spike delay against its solo latency.
- Reverberation after a single recall (e0, e2, e3): member spikes 15-450 ms after cue onset (a member re-fires after its first burst). Late activity: non-member pyramidal spikes in 0-450 ms; runaway = more than 50.
- "GABA silences reverberation" = with GABA on (x1, x2, x5) late member plus non-member spikes are lower than with SG at the same c and knob, and the last pyramidal spike comes earlier.
- Vm figures (PNG): recall event e0 (cue, non-cue and interneuron Vm, raster, GABA conductance on the non-cue cells reconstructed from interneuron spikes and connectome weights) for x1 vs SG vs x5; the two-cue event e1; a c = 3.0 event.

## Predictions (written before running; offline basis: interneurons 99, 107, 113 fire at about 6.2 ms for every memory at c 1.5 (headline_i raw), GABA kinetics 0.5/10 ms with 1 ms delay, veto level about 8-9 nS at a 2 ms lead (gaba_check_l model_veto))
1. V0: file and API programming give identical spikes (95%).
2. At c = 0 and 1.5 (NMDA off), single recalls do not reverberate in any GABA condition: each member fires once, 0 late member spikes, 0 non-member spikes, last pyramidal spike at about 4.5 ms (85%). So GABA has nothing to silence there, and SG = x1 = x2 = x5 for single recalls (80%).
3. At c = 3.0, late non-member activity is present with SG (more than 10 spikes in total over e0/e2/e3: 75%). It falls with GABA strength (x1 below SG, x5 below x1: 70%), but x5 does not reduce it to 0 (60%).
4. Second-cue discard, x1: no discard at delta 20 or 50 in any pair (90%). At delta 5 at most the pairs that end on G0 (G1->G0, G2->G0: G0's second non-cue cell receives about 7.4 nS) are discarded (each 40%). At delta 10, at most 1 of 6 pairs (75%).
5. x2: discards at delta 5 in 2-4 of 6 pairs, at delta 10 in 0-2, none at 20/50 (60%).
6. x5: discards at delta 5 in at least 5 of 6 pairs (70%), at delta 10 in 2-5 (60%), at delta 20 in 0-2 (70%), none at 50 (90%).
7. SG: no discards at any delta (90%): any failure with SG would come from AHP/adaptive threshold of shared cells or of the interneurons, not GABA (memories are disjoint, so the pyramidal cells are different).
8. Knobs (c = 1.5): AHP x0/x2 and ADP x0/x3 change no single-recall outcome (each memory still fires once) (70%) and change discard counts by at most 1 pair (65%). NMDA r 0.56 / 1.0 adds late member or non-member spikes (reverberation appears) in at least 1 of 3 solo recalls (45% / 60%); if it does, GABA x5 reduces it relative to SG (70%). AMPA x1.25: no reverberation at c 1.5 (65%). Lateral inhibition: more interneurons fire per recall (more than 3: 70%) and discards at delta 5-10 rise relative to the same GABA factor without it (60%).
9. Overall: the natural (x1) late GABA does what Randal describes only in a narrow window (delta at most about 10 ms) and only for some memory pairs. Silencing reverberation is untestable at c at most 1.5 without NMDA, because nothing reverberates (70%).

## Honest limits known in advance
One model, one memory design (3 memories), one deterministic simulation per condition (no noise), forced cue spikes. Interneurons are recruited (not driven). "Discard" is judged by completion of 2 non-cue cells. The file-programming route must pass V0 or the knob runs are void.
