## 1. Common constraints (identical to lp_weights; enforced in the solver, not checked afterwards)
Existing pairs only (graph variants below only REMOVE pairs or move/cap existing values, never add a pair beyond what a null model defines and says so); Dale: all variables are excitatory outputs x >= 0, GABA pairs untouched; weight w = x / G in [0.1, 1.0] (variants that delete pairs set w = 0 on the deleted pairs and are flagged "needs w = 0"); per-pair cap x <= min(G, 6.86 nS) (Randal's `autoassociative_connectome.py:75-79`); per-cell input cap sum_i x_ij <= max(3T = 49.5 nS, 0.1 sum_i G_ij) (my choice; hub rule D2 of lp_weights); T = 16.5 nS, unintended-input limit theta = 12.375 nS; objective: smallest, most uniform weights (waterfill). Variants: **primary** (all of the above, hub cells excluded as targets) and **Hinf** (no per-cell input cap; post-hoc in lp_weights, preregistered here as a sensitivity variant and flagged non-biological wherever a cell exceeds the cap).

## 2. Baseline and the success rule
Baseline = `lp_weights` primary medians over the 10 models: six-cell (4 cue + 2 non-cue) k = 0: 1.5, k <= 1: 2, k <= 3: 2; eight-cell: 0.
**A method gives a "biologically plausible capacity gain" if, in the PRIMARY variant, the median over the 10 models of the number of memories is at least 3x the baseline of its family (k = 0: >= 5; k <= 1 or k <= 3: >= 6) AND at least 8 of 10 models are at or above the baseline AND no hard flag below is raised.** The Hinf variant never counts as a gain (it breaks the input cap by construction).
Hard flags (non-biological, disqualifying): any cell above its input cap in the primary variant; any weight outside [0.1, 1.0] (or 0 on a deleted pair for variants that declare it); any per-pair x above 6.86 nS; a memory whose active fraction (cue + non-cue cells over 96) exceeds 25%; more than 50% of designed pairs on the upper weight bound (w >= 0.9); a design that needs deleted pairs but is counted as if it did not. Soft flags (reported, not disqualifying): active fraction above 10% (my assumption of CA3 sparsity; to be checked in the biology round), designed pairs with 5 or more synapses above 20% of designed pairs (unusual against published 1-2 contacts per CA3-CA3 connection and 1.85-4.5 per pair elsewhere), weight distribution not lognormal-like.
If no method meets the rule the biology round (`bio_scout/BIO_SCOUT.md`) is started.

## 4. Part 2: degree-controlled null graphs (same packer, same constraints)
Graph types for each of the 10 models (seeds 1, 2, 3 for random types; the packer uses 5 restarts):
- **grown**: the E4 graph.
- **ER**: the existing edge values (G) placed on uniformly random distinct ordered pairs (same number of edges; no hubs, in-degree about binomial; synapse-number distribution preserved).
- **CM**: directed configuration model: double-edge swaps (10 x |E| attempts, no self-loops or multi-edges) preserve every cell's in- and out-degree; each edge keeps its G value.
- **VS**: value shuffle: topology kept, the G values permuted among the existing edges (breaks the link between synapse number and position, keeps hubs).
- **CAPr**: hub-capped: D_cap = ceil(mean in-degree + 3 x sqrt(95 p (1-p))) (about 40; what a hub-free random graph of the same density would show as its maximum); each cell with in-degree above D_cap loses random in-edges down to D_cap (these edges become w = 0: flagged).
- **CAPs**: as CAPr but the weakest in-edges are removed first (strongest kept).
Families and variants: six-cell k = 0, k <= 1, k <= 3, eight-cell k = 0; primary and Hinf. Measures: number of memories, candidate cue sets, distinct target cells.
Predictions: P2.1 ER: zero candidate cue sets in at least 9 of 10 models (85%). P2.2 CM: Hinf k <= 1 count at most 25% of the grown count in at least 8 of 10 models (70%). P2.3 VS: same, at most 25% in at least 8 of 10 (65%). P2.4 CAPr: at most 50% of grown in at least 8 of 10 (70%); CAPs: at least 50% of grown in at least 6 of 10 (55%). P2.5 No null or capped graph reaches the success rule of section 2 (90%). The overall question answered: do hubs (heterogeneous in-degree) help or hurt capacity under this rule? My expectation: they help (they are where the strong pairs sit).

## 5. Part 1: distributed-cue assemblies (same constraints)
Memory = c cue cells + 2 non-cue targets; c in {8, 12, 17, 20}. Recall conditions in the threshold model: (i) the full cue gives every target sum >= T = 16.5 nS; (ii') any sub-cue that lacks 25% of the cue cells (q = ceil(0.75 c) cells present) gives every other member <= theta = 12.375 nS, i.e. the q largest contributions of every target sum to <= 12.375 (for c = 4 this is the "3 of 4 must not fire" rule of lp_weights); a cue cell gets at most theta from the other cue cells; (iii) the whole memory gives every cell outside it <= theta (no false completion, no spurious spikes); overlaps of at most k = 0 or 2 shared member cells; per-cell input cap (primary) and per-pair cap as in section 1. Weights are the minimal ("waterfill") solution x_c = clip(lambda, 0.1 G_c, min(G_c, 6.86)) with sum 16.5 for each target (no LP; DEVIATIONS D1), the largest requirement taking over where a pair serves two memories; they are verified independently (bounds, cap, cascade model with full cue exact and sub-cues silent, thresholds 16.5 and 15.0). Candidates: for every pair of target cells, the cue sets are the c best common input cells by min(U) plus 4 random weighted subsets.
Information note: c = 20 is 21-23% of the 96 cells active; c = 8: 10.4%.
Predictions: P1.1 primary, c = 8 and 12: median memories (k = 0) at most 3 (65%), i.e. no 3x gain, because each target still needs 16.5 nS of designed input and the input cap allows two memories per target. P1.2 c = 17 and 20, k = 0: median at most 4 (80%); a hard bound is the cell budget (96 / (c + 2): 4 memories at c = 20, 5 at c = 17, 9 at c = 8, 6 at c = 12). P1.3 Hinf counts exceed primary counts by a factor of at least 3 for some c (60%). P1.4 the independent check finds no violation (95%). P1.5 under the success rule no c qualifies (85%).
