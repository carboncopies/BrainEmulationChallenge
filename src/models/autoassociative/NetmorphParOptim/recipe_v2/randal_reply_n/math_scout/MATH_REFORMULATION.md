# Can the problem be recast so the solution space can be characterised or searched directly? (2026-11-25)

Literature + math only: no sims, no NES/API call, no emails, no git, no Drive. Earlier scouts (`math_scout/SCOUT.md`, `math_scout2/SCOUT2.md`) already covered Willshaw/Gardner, perceptron design, pruning and growth-parameter fitting at abstract level; this note recasts the *problem itself*, checks the theorems, and ranks experiments. Four literature agents read the sources; I read their extracts, not every paper myself (see "Evidence" below).

## 0. Short answer
1. **The weight side already is a known problem: a bank of independent per-neuron perceptrons with a fixed sparse support, sign (Dale) constraints, box bounds and an L1 (input-cap) bound** (Gardner 1988; Brunel 2004/2016; Clopath & Brunel 2013; Chapeton et al. 2015). In the threshold model every constraint we use (completion, silence for a 25% sub-cue, no cross-triggering, per-pair box, per-cell input cap) is linear in the in-weights of ONE target cell. So the feasible set is a **product of per-cell polytopes of dimension = in-degree (about 10-33 here), not one polytope of thousands of dimensions.** That makes exact LP margins, exact feasibility counting and hit-and-run volume estimates affordable. (DERIVED by me from `lp_weights/README.md` section 2; the product structure holds for the threshold model only, not once NMDA, adaptation and inhibition are in.)
2. **Classical capacity formulas do not transfer as numbers.** They assume dense random patterns with many active inputs per cell (f·k_i large). With n = 96, f = 5-18% and in-degree 20-30, a cell sees about 1-5 active inputs per pattern. So the formulas (2 per connection unconstrained, about half with sign constraints, (p|ln p|)^-1 for sparse coding) are scaling references, not predictions. No theorem was found for capacity on a fixed, hub-heavy graph with Dale, bounded weights and sparse patterns; the fixed-graph papers are simulations of Hebbian Hopfield nets.
3. **A short argument explains our main experimental finding and shows why "complete from 50-75% cues, then stop" cannot be had from excitation alone** (DERIVED, section 3): in a cooperative (non-negative) system with equal completion and sustain thresholds, completion from a q-fraction cue forces self-sustained ignition unless q > 1 - 1/k. Termination needs a non-cooperative variable (adaptation, delayed inhibition) and the gap between completion and sustain levels is what dynamics (NMDA hysteresis, adaptation) provide. This matches the stored data (completion of 75% cues at rho about 15.8 nS, ignition at about 17.4 nS, no window for 50% cues).
4. **What is worth doing first** (all offline, hours not days): per-cell LP margin and exact feasibility curves on the 10 grown graphs (candidate 1); exact ILP / branch-and-bound for assemblies and packing to replace greedy lower bounds (candidate 2); a spectral design rule with a convex formulation and a contraction certificate for the post-adaptation regime (candidate 3). Bayesian optimisation / simulation-based inference on the growth parameters is **not feasible at our evaluation budget** (candidate 9).

## 2. Candidate-by-candidate
Evidence tags: **V-FT** full text read by an agent, **V-AB** abstract only, **SEC** second-hand, **DER** my derivation, **UNV** not verified. Replica / cavity / mean-field results are non-rigorous.

### 2.1 Gardner volume, Hopfield, Willshaw (capacity of the weight space)
**Mapping.** For target cell i with in-neighbours N_i (fixed by growth), a memory mu with active set S_mu gives input x_mu = sum_{j in S_mu ∩ N_i} w_ij. Completion: x_mu >= theta for members i that are non-cue under every admissible cue; silence: sub-cue input < theta; non-members: whole-pattern input < theta. These are perceptron constraints with a margin, w_ij in [w_lo, w_hi], sum_j w_ij <= 49.5 nS, w >= 0.
**What the theory says.**
- Unconstrained perceptron: alpha_c = 2 patterns per input at zero margin (Gardner 1988 J Phys A 21:257; Gardner & Derrida 1988 J Phys A 21:271; replica-symmetric, non-rigorous; the margin-dependent formula alpha_c(kappa) = [∫_{-kappa}^{∞} Dt (kappa+t)^2]^-1 was only seen in a later paper, Zhong et al. arXiv:2206.08933) (V-AB for Gardner-Derrida; SEC for the formula).
- Sign-constrained weights halve it: capacity "is exactly one half" (Amit, Campbell, Wong 1989 J Phys A 22:4687, V-AB; the learning-algorithm paper is Amit, Wong, Campbell 1989 J Phys A 22:2039, whose algorithm "converges under the same conditions as required for an unconstrained perceptron", V-AB). Brunel 2016 Nat Neurosci 19:749 (V-FT main text): alpha_c = 1 at rho = 0 and about 0.14 at rho = 4 for a fully connected network (N = 800, f = 0.5), with at least half of the weights zero at capacity. Clopath & Brunel 2013 PLoS Comput Biol 9:e1002919 (V-FT): S + alpha_c = 1, alpha_c = 0.5 for the analog excitatory perceptron. Brunel et al. 2004 Neuron 43:745 eq. 27 is the sparse 0/1 formula (V-FT).
- Closest framework to ours: Chapeton, Fares, LaSota, Stepanyants 2015 Front Comput Neurosci 9:74 (the follow-up; the 2012 PNAS paper's full text was blocked): per-neuron perceptron, Dale sign constraints, total-weight (L1) cap (V-FT of the 2015 paper, abstract-only of the 2012 PNAS).
- Sparse coding: alpha_c ~ (p|ln p|)^-1 (Tsodyks & Feigelman 1988 Europhys Lett 6:101, V-AB); Treves & Rolls 1991 Network 2:371 (V-FT, OCR noisy): same scaling, capacity independent of connectivity for sparse coding; their CA3 example alpha_c ≈ 3, 36,000 memories at C = 12,000. The prefactor "0.2-0.3" that circulates was not found on the page read.
- Willshaw: 0.69 bits per synapse (ln 2) only for k ~ log n active units and very large n (SEC via Knoblauch et al. 2010); Willshaw et al. 1969 Nature 222:960 itself was not opened.
- Dilution: Derrida, Gardner, Zippelius 1987 Europhys Lett 4:167 (V-FT): alpha_c = 2/pi per connection for the Hebb rule with extreme asymmetric dilution (C << log N, N → ∞ first). That is a Hebb-rule number, not an optimal-weight bound (optimal is 2 per connection). Our own `math_run` already found that this reference is not an upper bound for our rule.
- Fixed graph: McGraw & Menzinger 2003 Phys Rev E 68:047102, Stauffer et al. 2003 Eur Phys J B 32:395, Torres et al. 2004, Kim 2004 are Hebbian Hopfield simulations with dense ±1 patterns (V-FT for McGraw-Menzinger, abstract for the rest). Qualitative agreement only: random / low-clustering graphs are best per synapse; hubs retrieve well while low-degree nodes err. **No theorem for a fixed heterogeneous graph with Dale, bounded weights and sparse patterns was found.**
- Solution geometry: Baldassi, Ingrosso, Lucibello, Saglietti, Zecchina 2015 PRL 115:128101 (V-FT, non-rigorous): for binary synapses the solution space is dominated by isolated solutions but dense accessible clusters exist; Cover 1965 IEEE Trans Electron Comput 14:326 (counting; the formula was seen on a secondary page only). With continuous weights each per-neuron solution set is a convex polytope, so the dense-cluster story applies only if weights are quantised.
**What it gives.** Existence and per-cell capacity scaling are LP facts; capacity per cell is about k_i (Dale) to 2 k_i (unconstrained) *when patterns are dense random* (DER, from the cited results; outside the stated regime for us, see 0.2). The joint capacity of a graph is limited by its low-in-degree cells.
**What it would change.** We stop treating "capacity of the grown graph" as a packing outcome of a particular greedy rule; we get P_c(graph) from exact LPs and can compare it with the formulas as a calibration, and the margin kappa* (max-margin LP) says how much room NMDA/adaptation can eat.
**Cost / risk.** Offline, cheap. Risk: it measures threshold-model feasibility, which under-predicted simulator completion in `partial_cue` (W70: threshold model 1/6 vs simulator 5/6).
**Cheap offline tests (not run).**
1. For each of the 10 grown graphs and each cell i, take random k = 8 patterns (cue 4-6 of 8), solve the per-cell max-margin LP under the NES constraints, and plot the fraction of cells feasible vs number of patterns P; read off P_c per graph and compare with in-degree k_i and with the Dale half-capacity formula.
2. Replace random patterns by the strong-pair candidate cue sets already in `math_run`; per cell, report the margin kappa* and how many cells have kappa* < 0 (the binding cells).
3. Control: the same LPs on degree-preserving rewired graphs (`math_run` nulls) to separate degree heterogeneity from wiring.

### 2.2 Sparse graphs, cliques, expanders, bootstrap percolation
**Mapping.** Completion is a threshold cascade on the weighted directed graph (cell fires when summed weight from active cells >= theta); an assembly is a dense strong subgraph; cross-triggering is an unwanted cascade; packing disjoint assemblies is set packing.
**Results (all verified at least at abstract level).**
- Bootstrap percolation: Janson, Luczak, Turova, Vallier 2012 Ann Appl Probab 22:1989 (V-FT, rigorous): for G(n,p) with count threshold r, a_c = (1 - 1/r)((r-1)!/(n p^r))^{1/(r-1)} valid only for sparse p << n^{-1/r}; Amini 2010 J Stat Phys 141:459 (V-FT, rigorous, directed configuration model, count threshold): Theorem 1 gives the final firing fraction as the root of f_alpha(y) = lambda y - (1-alpha) E[D_out 1(Bin(D_in, 1-y) < Omega)]; Amini & Fountoulakis 2014 J Stat Phys 155:72 (abstract): with heavy-tailed degrees a sublinear seed already spreads; Baxter et al. 2010 Phys Rev E 82:011103 (V-FT, non-rigorous, tree-like, per-vertex integer thresholds); Watts 2002 PNAS 99:5766 and Gleeson & Cahalane 2007 Phys Rev E 75:056103 (abstracts only); Chalupa-Leath-Reich 1979 (UNV); Balogh-Pittel 2007 (abstract). None gives a weighted-sum (nS) threshold result for a dense n = 96 directed graph.
- Linear threshold influence: Kempe, Kleinberg, Tardos 2003 KDD / 2015 Theory of Computing (V-FT) and Mossel & Roch 2010 SIAM J Comput 39:2176 (V-FT): with **uniform random thresholds and in-weights summing to at most 1** the expected spread is submodular, so greedy gets (1-1/e); with fixed thresholds it is NP-hard to approximate within n^(1-eps) (Kempe Thm 3.3). Our nS weights are not normalised and theta is fixed, so neither theorem applies to completion.
- Dense subgraphs: Charikar 2000 APPROX (V-FT): greedy peeling is a 2-approximation of average-degree density, exactly solvable by LP, directed version |E(S,T)|/sqrt(|S||T|); no size constraint, so hubs dominate. Bhaskara et al. 2010 STOC (V-FT): n^(1/4+eps) for densest k-subgraph using small-subgraph counts against a G(n,p) null; Feige-Peleg-Kortsarz 2001 Algorithmica 29:410 (SEC); planted clique: gap sqrt(n) vs log n is asymptotic (Alon-Krivelevich-Sudakov 1998 and Deshpande-Montanari 2015, V-FT); Peeters 2003 Discrete Appl Math 131:651 (one-line abstract: max edge biclique NP-complete). At n = 96 exact search is feasible in principle (DER; no source gives solve times).
- Spectra: Chung, Lu, Vu 2003 PNAS 100:6313 (V-FT draft): lambda_1 ≈ d~ = sum w^2 / sum w when d~ is large; undirected and asymptotic. At in-degree CV about 1 d~ is about 2x the mean degree (DER). Directed Cheeger (Chung 2005 Ann Comb 9:1, V-FT): 2h >= lambda >= h^2/2 bounds the Laplacian gap, not the weight-matrix dominant eigenvalue (loose relevance).
- Growth-side graph models: Abbe 2018 JMLR 18(177) (V-FT) weak-recovery threshold (a-b)^2 > 2(a+b) is asymptotic; Song et al. 2005 PLoS Biol 3:e68: reciprocal pairs about 4x random; Perin et al. 2011 PNAS 108:5419: no hubs; Reimann et al. 2017 Front Comput Neurosci 11:48 (read via a web summariser, SEC). Penrose 2003 and Holland et al. 1983: UNV (book not opened / secondary).
**What it gives.** Mostly hardness/threshold facts that either do not apply (asymptotic, tree-like, normalised weights) or are not needed at n = 96. The useful transfer: (i) hubs make cascades spread easily (Amini-Fountoulakis), consistent with our finding that hubs help capacity and cross-trigger risk; (ii) a degree-preserving null, not G(n,p), is the right comparison for subgraph counts (Bhaskara et al. use G(n,p), which our 10^7-10^9 excess ratios already show is wrong for grown graphs).
**What it would change.** Mainly interpretation and the null model; a directed-configuration-model cascade calculation could predict the cue size at which a *spurious* cascade out of an assembly percolates, bounding cross-triggering without simulation.
**Cost / risk.** Offline; risk that the sparse/tree-like assumptions fail at n = 96, where simulation of the cascade on the real graph is cheaper than any formula.
**Cheap offline tests (not run).**
1. Exact max-weight biclique/clique and densest-k-subgraph on each grown graph with an off-the-shelf solver (n = 96); compare with the greedy packer counts in `math_run`.
2. Cascade Monte Carlo on each grown graph with the true nS weights: seed fraction vs final spread for random seeds and for assembly-member seeds; compare with Amini's configuration-model prediction using the graph's in/out degree sequence.
3. Compute rho of the strong-pair subgraph per candidate assembly and compare with Chung-Lu-Vu d~ (a check on how much of the dominant eigenvalue is explained by degrees).

### 2.3 Threshold-linear, Perron-Frobenius, monotone systems, contraction (stability and termination)
**Mapping.** Rate form x' = -x + [W x - theta]_+ with W >= 0 on the member subgraph; ignition = existence of a second stable fixed point; termination needs the active state to lose stability.
**Results.**
- Curto et al. graph rules (Curto, Geneson, Morrison 2019 Neural Comput 31:1; Curto & Morrison 2023 Notices AMS; V-FT/abstract): all are for **competitive (W <= 0)** networks; their "cliques" are weakly-inhibiting sets under global inhibition, not excitatory cliques, so no certificate for non-negative W. What transfers: a support sigma is stable iff all eigenvalues of -I + W_sigma have negative real part, which for W_sigma >= 0 means rho(W_sigma) < 1 (the Perron step is DER). Hahnloser, Seung, Slotine 2003 Neural Comput 15:621 Theorem 1 (symmetric W) reduces to rho < 1 for non-negative symmetric W.
- Monotone systems: with W >= 0 the flow is cooperative (Hirsch & Smith 2005 Handbook of Differential Equations, V-FT) and generic orbits converge to equilibria, so **a purely excitatory network cannot oscillate stably; termination needs a non-cooperative slow variable** (adaptation, delayed inhibition). Krasnosel'skii sublinearity rules out bistability for concave nonlinearities (Thm 5.22 as cited).
- Contraction: Kozachkov, Ennis, Slotine 2022 NeurIPS (arXiv 2106.08928, V-FT), Theorem 1: P(g|W| - I) + (g|W| - I)^T P ≺ 0 for some positive diagonal P gives contraction; for W >= 0 this is equivalent to g·rho(W) < 1 (Hurwitz Metzler matrices are diagonally stable; my reading, DER). A contracting circuit has fading memory, so it certifies the post-adaptation regime only. Time-varying gain: only their Theorem 6 (slowly varying slopes); no paper treats adaptation as an explicit state. Rate-model result, not conductance spiking. Lohmiller & Slotine 1998 Automatica 34:683 (preprint read).
- Inhibition: ISN condition gain·W_EE > 1 (Sanzeni et al. 2020 eLife 9:e54875, V-FT appendix; the garbled formula A12 unchecked), the same threshold as bistability; Tsodyks et al. 1997, Murphy & Miller 2009, Ozeki et al. 2009, Roudi & Latham 2007, Brunel 2000, Amit & Brunel 1997 (title: "Model of global spontaneous activity and local structured activity during delay periods in the cerebral cortex", Cereb Cortex 7:237): abstracts only; no abstract read says inhibition terminates a retrieval.
- Adaptation-terminated lifetime: the only explicit formula is McCleney & Kilpatrick 2016 J Math Biol 73:1131 (arXiv 1504.06290, V-FT; Heaviside rate model): T_up = tau ln[(phi - I)/(phi - alpha - I)] with alpha recurrent strength, phi adaptation strength, I drive; it diverges as adaptation fails to beat recurrence. Roach, Sander, Zochowski 2016 Phys Rev E 93:052307: adaptation shifts Hopfield attractor stability (period about 4 tau). Compte et al. 2003, Holcman & Tsodyks 2006, Mejias et al. 2010, Treves 1993: no lifetime formula.
- Spectral radius control: Kingman 1961 (Q J Math 12:283; seen via Nussbaum 1986, SEC): if entries of A are log-convex in t then r(A) is log-convex in t, so **log rho(exp(U)) is convex in U**; Agrawal, Diamond, Boyd (arXiv 1812.04074, V-FT): the Perron eigenvalue is log-log convex and minimising it is a geometric-program example. I spot-checked log-convexity numerically on 2,000 random 17x17 sparse matrices (midpoint convexity of log rho(exp(U)), zero violations; this is a check, not a proof). A bound rho <= b is convex in log-weights; rho >= a is not, so a window [a, b] is difference-of-convex; **but hitting a rho target needs no optimisation: rho(cW) = c rho(W), so rescale W to the target** (optimisation is needed only under per-weight box constraints). Our linear "sum of weights >= theta" completion constraints are reverse-posynomial in log-weights, so a pure GP cannot hold both; a fixed-Perron-vector linear inner bound (from the min-characterisation of rho) is the workable route (agent's reading). Boyd 2007 GP tutorial has no Perron content.
**Derived result for us (DER, not from a paper).** Take a uniform symmetric assembly of k cells with pair weight w and equal completion and sustain thresholds theta. Completion from a q-fraction cue needs q k w >= theta; being silent after completion (no self-sustain) needs (k-1) w < theta. Both hold only if q > (k-1)/k. For q = 0.75 and k = 17 the second condition is violated, so completion from 75% cues implies self-sustained ignition. A window exists only through hysteresis (sustain threshold above completion threshold: NMDA nonlinearity, adaptation timing, delayed inhibition). The measured window is about 10% of rho (15.8 vs 17.4 nS), none for 50% cues: this is what the argument predicts. Non-uniform (designated cue->target) designs avoid the argument by completing from specific cue sets only, which is why the K(4,2) R3 designs work and 17-cell any-subset assemblies ignite.
**What it gives.** Constructive: scale to a rho window; convex formulation for rho caps; a post-adaptation contraction certificate (rho_eff < 1 once the adapted threshold has risen); a lifetime scaling T_up ~ tau ln(...) to compare with our 280-440 ms (the formula is for a Heaviside rate model; our stored data show lifetime nearly independent of c above threshold, so it will not fit directly).
**What it would change.** It turns the previous "rho design rule" hypothesis into a convex/ scaling problem with an explicit trade-off statement, and says what must be added (an inhibitory or adaptation-based non-cooperative term) rather than tuning excitation further.
**Cost / risk.** Offline. Risk: rate-model certificates may not carry over to conductance spiking with NMDA (no source covers it); treat as predictions to test in the existing reduced model (`loopedwsp_scout/stability/rn.py`).
**Cheap offline tests (not run).**
1. In `rn.py`, vary the completion/sustain gap by scaling NMDA and fit the window width vs NMDA fraction; check whether the window closes as NMDA goes to 0 (the DER argument says it should shrink to q > 1 - 1/k).
2. For the 14 stored arm/model matrices compute rho and the post-adaptation contraction margin g_eff·rho with the adapted threshold (+1 mV/spike, tau 500 ms) and compare with the observed termination times.
3. Solve the log-weight convex program (minimise rho subject to the linear completion constraints, in the fixed-Perron-vector inner bound) and report the minimum rho attainable for a 75%-cue-completing design per graph; if it exceeds 17.4 nS for every graph, the threshold-model design cannot complete and stay silent.

### 2.4 Convex relaxation (LP / SDP / quadratic constraints) for weight design
**Mapping.** Per-cell LP (candidate 2.1) plus an optional spectral constraint; cross-cell terms only through the dynamics. **Results.** Convex Optimization (Boyd & Vandenberghe 2004) is the generic reference (not opened; textbook); Candes & Tao 2005 IEEE Trans Inf Theory 51:4203 is "Decoding by Linear Programming" (abstract and opening only): recovery guarantees need random or incoherent matrices, so L1 minimisation to pick a *sparse* set of strengthened pairs is a heuristic here, not a guaranteed method. We already use LP (`lp_weights`) and showed it cannot raise single-memory capacity (an algebraic identity there); it removes loops that R3 programmed. **Change:** none new for feasibility; value is a margin objective (max-min slack) and the sparse-pair objective. **Cost/risk:** offline, trivial. **Test:** add a max-min-slack objective to the per-cell LP of 2.1 and an L1-sparsity variant; report slack distribution and number of strengthened pairs.

### 2.5 Polytope sampling and solution-space volume
**Mapping.** The per-cell feasible sets of 2.1 are convex polytopes in dimension k_i; log-volume of the joint feasible set is the sum over cells (product structure, DER, threshold model only).
**Results.** Exact volume is #P-hard; Dyer, Frieze, Kannan 1991 J ACM 38:1 (polynomial randomised approximation; not opened). Cousins & Vempala, Math Prog Comp 8:133-160 (2016) (V-FT): about 100 dimensions in about 10 minutes, "a few hundred" claimed; rounding becomes prohibitive beyond about 50 dimensions for non-round bodies. Chen, Dwivedi, Wainwright, Yu 2018 JMLR 19 (V-FT): hit-and-run mixing about d^2 gamma^2 (gamma = condition number of the set), versus Dikin walk O(nd). CHRR (Haraldsdottir, Cousins, Thiele, Fleming, Vempala 2017 Bioinformatics 33:1741, V-FT): samples polytopes of dimension 24 to 2,430 after rounding. Lovasz & Vempala 2006 SIAM J Comput 35:985 (abstract): hit-and-run mixes from any interior point (the O*(n^3) figure is from their technical report, not the corner paper's abstract). Smith 1984 Oper Res 32:1296 (abstract). Gutenkunst et al. 2007 PLoS Comput Biol 3:e189: sloppy spectra, so expect very elongated sets that need rounding. Prinz, Bucher, Marder 2004 Nat Neurosci 7:1345 and Marder & Taylor 2011 Nat Neurosci 14:133 (abstract level): degenerate solution families are the norm in neural circuits.
**What it gives.** An empirical measure of how many ways a grown graph can store a memory set (volume ratio vs the box), and of which pairs are pinned (near-zero range = essential). With dimension about 25 per cell it is inside the demonstrated range, unlike the full weight space of thousands of dimensions.
**What it would change.** Ranks grown graphs and cells by flexibility and identifies pairs whose weight is forced, to guide growth (which pairs need more synapses).
**Cost/risk.** Offline, moderate; the polytope can be very thin (sloppy), so check rounding and that the Chebyshev radius is not near 0.
**Tests (not run).** 1. Chebyshev radius (an LP) per cell as the cheap first proxy; 2. CHRR or hit-and-run with rounding on the 10 worst cells of one graph, report volume ratio vs box and per-pair ranges; 3. repeat after adding the 50%-cue completion constraint to see how fast the volume collapses (compare with the stored 50%-cue loss).

### 2.6 SAT / ILP / CSP for "which pairs to strengthen"
**Mapping.** Binary variables s_ij (pair strengthened) with linear threshold constraints per cell; assembly choice as a binary program. **Results.** Mezard & Montanari 2009 (book; not opened) for random CSP thresholds; Braunstein & Zecchina 2006 PRL 96:030201 and Baldassi, Braunstein, Brunel, Zecchina 2007 PNAS 104:11079 (abstracts only): message-passing learning in networks with discrete synapses; max clique exact methods: Tomita & Seki 2003 and Bron-Kerbosch 1973 (not opened); modern ILP (HiGHS, Huangfu & Hall 2018 Math Prog Comp 10:119; OR-Tools CP-SAT; neither benchmarked in what was read). **No source gives solve times at n = 96 (agent inference: easy; UNV).** **What it gives.** True optimum rather than greedy lower bounds; a proof when a graph cannot host more than m memories. **Change:** the "capacity" numbers in `math_run` and `fresh_learn` (greedy, best of 3-10 restarts) become exact; this settles whether the limit is the graph or the packer. **Cost/risk:** offline, low; formulation size can blow up for overlapping memories. **Tests (not run):** 1. exact max-weight clique and K(4,2) biclique enumeration on each graph and compare with greedy; 2. ILP for the disjoint packing with conflict graph over the enumerated candidates; 3. timing study at k = 6, 8, 12, 17 to replace the unsourced "easy at n = 96".

### 2.7 Matroid and submodular choice of memories
**Mapping.** Choose a family of memories maximising count subject to overlap limits = set packing (maximum independent set in a conflict graph), not a matroid problem. **Results.** Nemhauser, Wolsey, Fisher 1978 Math Program 14:265 ((1-1/e) greedy for monotone submodular under cardinality, standard); Calinescu, Chekuri, Pal, Vondrak 2011 SIAM J Comput 40:1740 ((1-1/e) under one matroid; publisher abstract only; does not apply to disjointness packing); set packing: Hurkens & Schrijver 1989 SIAM J Discrete Math 2:68, Chandra & Halldorsson 2001 J Algorithms 39:223, Hazan, Safra, Schwartz 2006 Comput Complexity 15:20 (guarantees from a survey, Oosterwijk arXiv 1507.07459, SEC): unweighted k-set packing k/2+eps (Hurkens-Schrijver), best known (k+1)/3+eps, weighted (k+1)/2, hardness Omega(k/ln k); for k = 6-17 the worst-case ratios are weak. Fisher-Nemhauser-Wolsey matroid-intersection 1/p: from memory only (UNV). **Verdict:** low value as theory; the exact ILP of 2.6 is the right tool at this size. Submodularity only helps with a random-threshold linear-threshold surrogate (2.2), which is not our rule.

### 2.8 Graph-theoretic design of connectivity (random geometric graphs, small world, SBM)
**Mapping.** The growth parameters set an effective connectivity kernel; we want graphs in which dense strong subgraphs (assemblies) are plentiful *and* findable. **Results.** See 2.2 (Penrose RGG: UNV; Holland SBM: SEC; Abbe: thresholds asymptotic; Song/Perin/Reimann: data on cortex, not CA3 design rules). Our own data (`math_run`, `morph_tests`): capacity comes from degree heterogeneity and a few hub cells; hubs are partly a finite-slab edge effect (Spearman -0.46 to -0.61 of in-degree with distance from the slab centre; post hoc); steering axons (fresh_run #3) raised own-group share to 0.27-0.31 (chance 0.053) but not enough for a usable assembly. **What it would change:** a degree-corrected block model fitted to the grown graphs gives a cheap generator for the *kind* of graph that stores memories, letting us ask which block structure (not which morphology parameter) is needed, then look for parameters that produce it. **Cost/risk:** offline, medium; no theory transfers at n = 96. **Tests (not run):** 1. fit a degree-corrected SBM with 8 blocks to the strong-pair graphs and compare strong-biclique counts with the real graphs (does block structure explain the excess over a configuration model?); 2. rewiring tests that keep in/out-degrees and the distance kernel (RGG-like) to separate geometry from degrees; 3. a slab-size sweep in the generator (not NES) to test the edge-effect explanation.

### 2.9 Bayesian optimisation, simulation-based inference, optimal transport (parameter side)
**Results.** Eriksson & Poloczek 2021 AISTATS (SCBO): traditional BO handles "at most 15" dimensions with "a couple of hundred samples"; their smallest benchmark is 100 evaluations or more (V-FT per agent). ## 3. What this recasting says about the experiments we have run
- **Limits are the graph, not the weight rule** (LP, `lp_weights`): consistent with the per-cell view: capacity of a cell is bounded by its in-degree and strong pairs, and the joint capacity by its worst cells.
- **Ignition = cooperative-system bistability**, set by rho (loop gain). Completion from q-fraction cues forces rho above the sustain level unless a hysteresis exists (DER above). The stored 10% window for 75% cues and the absent window for 50% cues are what that argument predicts.
- **Termination needs a non-cooperative element**; reset and sAHP tests in `neuron_reset_ahp` modify the adaptation (slow variable), consistent with the sAHP being the only knob that shortened reverberation (to 96 ms on one model at the preregistered setting, with a 50%-cue completion cost).
- **Fixed pair strengths cannot fit all graphs**: the sustain level is a spectral radius, a graph property; the scaling rho(cW) = c rho(W) makes per-graph calibration trivial offline.
