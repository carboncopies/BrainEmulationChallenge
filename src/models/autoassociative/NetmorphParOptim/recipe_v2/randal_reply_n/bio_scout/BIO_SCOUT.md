## 1. What real CA3 does differently (summary, each point sourced below)
1. **It is large and each cell has thousands of recurrent inputs; our patch has about 27.** About 12,000 recurrent-collateral synapses per CA3 pyramidal cell (Treves & Rolls 1992, snippet); 30-70% of a CA3 cell's synapses contact other CA3 cells (Li et al. 1994, as cited in Le Duigou et al. 2014, secondary). Capacity scales with the number of connections per cell (Treves & Rolls 1991; Roudi & Latham 2007; round 1), so our in-degree of 22-33 is about 400 times smaller than the anatomical figure (my arithmetic).
2. **Each recurrent connection is weak and has one or two contacts**, and connectivity is sparse: Guzman et al. 2016 (single contacts, unitary EPSPs about 0.56 mV, snippets); connectivity sparsifies from mouse to human, with cell number as the main determinant, and human CA3 synapses are more reliable (Watson et al. 2025; the 110,000 mouse and 1.7 million human pyramidal counts come from a conference-abstract snippet, secondary). So real recall relies on summation of **tens** of weak inputs, not four strong ones (my arithmetic: a 15 mV threshold depolarisation divided by 0.56 mV is about 27 coincident inputs if summation is linear; NMDA-spike supralinearity lowers this, item 4).
3. **Memories are sparse but overlapping, selected by the dentate gyrus.** About 18% of CA3 cells are active in a context (Arc, secondary; 8-23% in other Arc estimates), more different environments recruit less overlapping CA3 sets (Leutgeb et al. 2007), single granule-cell bursts "detonate" CA3 cells conditionally (Henze et al. 2002; Vyleta et al. 2016). Random overlap between two assemblies of active fraction a in N cells is about a^2 N cells (my arithmetic: 1 cell at a = 10%, N = 96).
4. **Pyramidal dendrites integrate supralinearly** (NMDA spikes in thin CA3 dendrites; Makara & Magee 2013), which changes how many inputs a cue must supply.
5. **Partial cues complete.** Wild-type mice keep CA1 place fields with three of four cues removed; CA3 NMDA-receptor knockouts do not (Nakazawa et al. 2002; the "3 of 4 removed" detail from a RIKEN summary). CA3 output is closer to the stored pattern than its degraded input (Neunuebel & Knierim 2014). That is the opposite of our recall rule, which forbids completion from a cue lacking 25% of its cells.
6. **Inhibition is mostly feed-forward onto CA3 from mossy-fibre filopodia and local loops** (Acsady et al. 1998; Pouille & Scanziani 2001; Lawrence & McBain 2003; Rebola et al. 2017), with plastic GABAergic connectivity.
7. **Skew is normal**: firing rates are lognormal-like with a minority of cells dominating (Mizuseki & Buzsaki 2013); strengths are lognormal-like (Song et al. 2005, round 2). Heavy-tailed activity is biological; heavy-tailed in-degree has no source here.

## 3. Ranking: what to try next inside the stated limits
1. **Decide the completion rule with Randal (B), then recompute (A).** Biology says partial cues complete and assemblies overlap by about a^2 N. If a rule like "50% of the cue completes, 25% must not" is acceptable, the threshold model already gives 10-17 memories at c = 8-12 under all the biological constraints (post-hoc numbers; to be preregistered and verified). Cost: offline.
2. **Biological cue size (A):** extend to c = 17-40 single-synapse cues with the matching completion rule; check weight and synapse statistics (single-contact share 45-48% at c = 20) against Guzman. Cost: offline, a day.
3. **Use the larger-D networks we already grew** (capacity_d, linefix_c: p 0.35-0.44) with the new distributed-cue machinery to see whether in-degree, not the design, limits the count (E). Cost: offline.
4. **Grow one set of larger slabs within the limits (radius 200, thickness 50, 128 pyramidal)** to test whether the hubs are an edge effect (math_run found in-degree falls with distance from the slab centre, rho about -0.5): needs sims and the arbor-seeds slots; not allowed now.
5. **DG-like sparse drive and feed-forward inhibition (C, F)** only after 1-3, because they need new model structure or sims.
Not recommended: dendritic NMDA nonlinearity (D) through the current API; it would remove the programmed-pair interface our designs rely on.

## 4. What stays unresolved (honest list)
- I found no source for the cue size (number of cells) needed for completion in vivo; Nakazawa et al. report the fraction of external cues, not cells. Assembly sizes in cells were not retrieved (Harris et al. 2003 give a 10-30 ms assembly time window only).
- The Arc active fractions (18%, 8-23%) are snippet-level; the 12,000 recurrent synapses per cell and the 41-50 mossy-fibre inputs per CA3 cell are second-hand (Treves & Rolls 1992 snippet; Amaral et al. 1990 as cited by later papers).
- The mouse 110,000 and human 1.7 million CA3 pyramidal counts are from one conference-abstract snippet.
- Whether CA3 completion in vivo needs inhibition for stability, and how much, was not settled by these sources.
