Question (John): GABA on LIFC neurons works in other setups, so why does it show no response in our E4 autoassociative recall? Bug or biological-range strength?
Known before writing (read, not yet analysed for this task): NES LIFC neuron = conductance-based exponential Euler: g_L = 1/Rm = 10 nS (Rm 100 MOhm, Cm 100 pF, tau 10 ms), V_inf = (sum g_k E_k + g_L Vrest)/(g_L + sum g_k); Netmorph-built principal AND interneuron: Vrest -70, threshold -50, reset -55; GABA receptor E_rev -70 (NetmorphManagerThread.cpp:360), rise 0.5, decay 10 ms, 0.8 nS x weight 0.2 per synapse. Reference model with working GABA: bec-tooling full_adder_lifc.py (GABA PeakConductance 40 nS, weight 0.5-2.0, decay 13 ms; Vrest -70; passes its truth table).
Hypotheses (H = candidate cause of invisibility), each with the evidence that would settle it:
- H1 reversal = resting (no driving force at rest, so GABA only shunts). Test: IPSP at rest ~0 mV; IPSP size grows with depolarisation toward -50.
- H2 per-pair strength far below the reference (E4 Int->Pyr per pair 0.16 n nS, n ~ 4-5 synapses, i.e. ~0.7 nS; reference 40+ nS per connection, ratio >= 50x). Test: summed GABA conductance at a target vs the 16.5 nS excitatory R3 threshold.
- H3 timing: interneurons are driven feed-forward and fire after the Pyr members already fired. Test: interneuron first-spike latency vs first non-cue Pyr spike in existing raw spike files.
- H4 targets do not overlap with cells that would fire falsely / too few interneurons fire.
- H5 a bug (GABA not loaded or silenced by mistake). Test: connectome shows GABA pairs with weight 0.2 and GPeakSum, silenced run zeroes them (headline_i load check already records gaba_weights), and a direct unit test shows a conductance-driven Vm change.
