# gaba_check_l: why does GABA show no response in our E4 autoassociative recall? (2026-11-20)
**Verdict: not a bug. GABA is loaded and acts, and a hand-built reference shows it clearly. In our recall it is invisible because it arrives too late, not because it is too weak.** The inhibition in the E4 network is feed-forward: interneurons are driven by the Pyr volley and fire first at a median 6.2 ms after cue onset (range 5.3-9.5 ms, 2,688 events, never before 5.3 ms), but the programmed non-cue members fire at 4.5 ms. A veto has to arrive within about 3 ms of cue onset (E4 unit test: forced interneuron spikes at 0-3 ms veto the memory, at 4 ms or later never, whatever the number of interneurons (1, 3 or all 20) and whatever the strength (x1, x2, x2.5)). Two further facts make the GABA invisible even when it is in time: (1) the reversal potential equals the resting potential (-70 mV both), so GABA produces exactly 0 mV at rest and only acts on a depolarised cell (shunt/hyperpolarisation grows with depolarisation); (2) the grown per-pair strength is 0.57-0.82 nS on average (0.16 nS x 3.6-5.1 synapses) against 40 nS in the reference model, so one natural pair delays a threshold-level target spike by only 0.06 ms (3.3 nS: 0.36 ms) and 7 nS is needed to veto. Where inhibition does have time to act (spurious firing later in a trial: background c = 1.5 and 3.0 nS) it clearly works: spurious non-member Pyr cells at c = 1.5 are 2 with GABA on vs 683 silenced (all 465 x 2 full-cue events), and at c = 3.0 spurious spikes drop from 545/323/796/1,336/780/855/414 to 14/220/543/737/412/149/207 (GABA silenced to on, headline_i). That is the "inhibition only matters at c = 1.5-3.0" that John saw; it is the expected signature of late, feed-forward inhibition.

## 1. Reference case: GABA clearly working on LIFC neurons
- Existing reference: bec-tooling `full_adder_lifc.py` (an XOR/full-adder built from a hand-made LIFC network; validation_results.md: all 7 test cases pass). GABA receptors there: E_rev -70, rise 0.5, decay 10-13 ms, PeakConductance 40 nS, Weight 0.1-2.0 (IA0_PB0 weight 2.0, decay 13), neurons Vrest -70 / threshold -50 (same as ours). I did not re-run that script; "GABA is needed for the XOR" is taken from its design, not tested by me.
- My own hand-built reference (`unit_ref.py`, 64 independent circuits in one simulation): target P (Netmorph principal LIFC defaults) + 4 AMPA drivers + 1 interneuron I with GABA onto P; kinetics as in the Netmorph build (AMPA 0.5/3 ms, GABA 0.5/10 ms, onset 1 ms), pair conductance G = PeakConductance x weight 1.0.
| test | G (nS) | result |
|---|---|---|
| I alone, P at rest | 0.66 ... 40 | Vm stays -70.000 mV (min = max = baseline) at every G: no driving force, no IPSP |
| AMPA volley 10 nS (subthreshold), I at same time | 0 / 0.66 / 3.3 / 14 / 40 | peak Vm -54.79 / -55.02 / -55.84 / -58.40 / -61.88 mV (GABA clearly reduces the depolarisation, in proportion to G) |
| AMPA volley 16.5 nS (R3 threshold), I at same time | 0 / 0.66 / 3.3 / 7 / 14 / 40 | P spike at 4.80 / 4.86 / 5.16 ms / **vetoed** (peak -50.07) / vetoed (-52.3) / vetoed (-57.4) |
| same, I 1 or 2 ms earlier | 7 | vetoed only when simultaneous; at 1 ms and 2 ms earlier it still fires (5.91, 5.66 ms); 14 nS vetoes at all three leads |
| AMPA volley 20 nS | 14 / 40 | spike at 4.99 ms / vetoed at 40 (peak -55.1) |
GABA therefore works in NES on LIFC neurons exactly as the code says (`update_membrane_potential_exponential_Euler_Cm`: V_inf = (sum g_k E_k + g_L V_rest)/(g_L + sum g_k), g_L = 10 nS, tau 10 ms). My offline numerical copy of that equation (nesmodel.py, `model_veto.json`) reproduces it: it fires from 14 nS (12.4 nS silent; the repo recipes say about 15 nS), vetoes a 16.5 nS volley with 7 nS at lead 0 (9 nS at 2 ms, 12 nS at 5 ms), and predicts the 40 nS peak -62 mV. Cross-check agreement is within the 1 nS grid.

## 3. E4 unit tests (grown model V1n20 s33333, `unit_e4.py`)
- **GABA loaded**: connectome shows 1,174 GABA pairs, weight set {0.2}; after the dyn2.py silencing 0 non-zero (and weights {0.0}).
- **One interneuron on its targets at rest (nat, W1)**: interneuron 104 (strongest pair 5.12 nS = 32 synapses) fires 10 spikes at 100 Hz; all its Pyr targets (the 6 largest checked: pair 5.12, 4.96, 3.52, 2.56, 2.24, 2.24 nS) stay at -70.000 mV in both GABA x1 and silenced runs: IPSP = 0.000 mV, i.e. no hyperpolarisation at rest (reversal = rest), not a missing synapse.
- **On depolarised targets (nat, W2)**: their Pyr presynaptic cells fire 3 spikes (targets then fire); with the 10-spike train the target fires 5/5/4/3/4/3 spikes with GABA x1 and 8/10/7/5/6/4 with GABA silenced (targets 29, 63, 4, 40, 33, 81): GABA shortens and weakens the response of a depolarised cell (25-50% fewer spikes), so it acts; its IPSP amplitude was not separated from the spike/AHP waveform in these traces.
- **Programmed memory (prog; R3 programming, D42 group 0, members 10, 11, 29, 34, 63, 78, non-cue 29, 63)**: cue alone: both non-cue members fire at 4.52 ms. Forcing k interneurons (strongest summed GABA first; rank-0 pair onto the non-cue cells 5.12 / 4.96 nS) to spike AT cue onset with GABA x1: k = 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 12, 20 all veto both (0 of 2); with GABA silenced they all fire (2 of 2; first spike 5.13-5.40 ms against 4.80 ms with no forced interneuron: a small delay that is present even with GABA silenced, cause not investigated).
- **Timing sweep (lead_sweep.py)**: interneuron spikes at DELTA ms after cue onset. Non-cue members fire (not vetoed) when:
| variant | k = 1 | k = 3 | k = 20 |
|---|---|---|---|
| x1 | vetoed at delta 0 and 2, fires (delayed 1.4 ms) at 1, fires at 3-8 | vetoed 0-3, fires from 4 | vetoed 0-3, fires from 4 |
| x2 | vetoed 0-3, fires from 4 | same | same |
| x2.5 | vetoed 0-3, fires from 4 | same | same |
The window closes at about 3 ms (a 1 ms synaptic delay + 4.5 ms spike); stronger GABA (x2, x2.5) makes k = 1 as reliable as k = 3 or 20 inside the window and does nothing outside it. The natural first interneuron spike (5.3-9.5 ms) is after the window in 100% of events (0 of 2,688 at <= 4 ms). The x1 k = 1 non-monotonic pattern (fires at delta 1, vetoed at 2) is a knife-edge of threshold crossing at 5.1 nS, not investigated further.

## 4. Where the effect is lost, and x2 / x2.5
Chain for the cue-completion race: (a) interneuron firing is present but sparse (2 of 20, mean 2.3) and feed-forward; (b) per-pair strength 0.57-0.82 nS (single natural pair delays the target spike by 0.06-0.4 ms, well below the 7 nS veto level, but the summed ceiling onto programmed targets is large); (c) **timing: the interneurons fire 1.7 ms (median) after the target already spiked: this is the step with the smallest ratio (no effect possible, 0 nS present)**; (d) the targets do overlap (the programmed non-cue members are GABA targets of the strongest interneurons: pair 5.12 / 4.96 nS), so geometry is not the limit; (e) rest = reversal gives exactly 0 mV at rest.
Does gaba_patch x2 / x2.5 change it? For cue completion in natural recall: no (every timing outcome above is the same for x1, x2, x2.5 once the interneuron spike is later than 4 ms; this agrees with gaba_patch's S1 finding that all metrics are identical within 0.5 ms). It changes the reliability only inside the 0-3 ms window (k = 1). For the late stage (runaway at c = 3.0) GABA already acts at x1 (spurious spikes 545 -> 14, 796 -> 543, ...), but it does not contain the runaway (> 50 spurious spikes in 4 of 5 models, headline_i); x2/x2.5 was not tested on that stage (c = 3.0 not simulated here), so I make no claim about it.

## 5. Bug or biological-range strength? (plain)
Not a bug: GABA is loaded (0.2 weight, 0.8 nS/synapse, E -70, 10 ms decay), the reference circuit and the E4 model both show clear GABA action (peak reduction, vetoes, 3-95% fewer spurious spikes when silenced), and the code computes a standard conductance-based membrane update. The invisibility in cue-completion recall comes from timing (feed-forward inhibition that follows the Pyr volley by about 2 ms) combined with rest = reversal and weak per-pair strength; the natural strength is at the low end of the literature (gaba_patch natural_range.json: mean unitary Int->Pyr 0.255 nS here vs Ecker 0.65; x2.54 reaches the mean; 13.7 GABA synapses per Pyr vs 60-140 natural terminals; decay 10 ms vs Ecker 3.3 ms), so there is room inside the biology to strengthen it but doing so would not fix the timing. `bio_ranges.json` has no GABA-strength entry (it holds growth parameters and D_synmax only: days, pyramidal, interneuron, minneuronseparation, shape.radius, shape.thickness, dm.weight, D_synmax x4), so "inside bio_ranges" for GABA strength means inside the gaba_patch natural range; no weight outside it was used except the diagnostic references (40 nS hand-built, x2 / x2.5 copies, k forced spikes), none of which is a recommendation.

## 7. Honest limits
- Unit tests rest on one model (s33333) and one programmed memory (D42 group 0, two non-cue cells), one deterministic simulation per cell; the veto window of about 3 ms is for that memory (threshold crossing at 4.5 ms, a 1 ms synaptic delay) and will move a little with memory design; I did not test other models or designs.
- Interneuron spikes in the unit tests are forced (SetSpecificAPTimes), not network-generated; the natural interneuron latencies (5.3-9.5 ms) come from the 294 ripple_overlap_j sims (2,790 full-cue events across 10 E4 models), where GABA was the build's 0.2 / 0.8 nS.
- The offline model is my reimplementation of the update equation without AHP/adaptive threshold and a fixed 1 ms synaptic delay; it matches the simulator at the 1-2 nS level (veto 7 nS in both for lead 0, but at lead 1-2 ms the simulator's 7 nS still fired where the model predicted 8-9 nS, consistent). The delay used for the "GABA on non-cue member at its spike" figure is 1 ms (OnsetDelay in the build; the per-receptor value in the model file was not read).
- The "hypothetical GABA at spurious cells" numbers use interneuron spikes from silenced runs and only include cells that fired (selection bias); they say how much inhibition could have been present, not that it would have prevented the spike.
- "GABA acts at c = 1.5 / 3.0" is an on-vs-silenced comparison on the existing raw files (c = 1.5: 2 vs 683 spurious cells; c = 3.0: spurious spikes per run listed in section 0), not a new experiment, and "silenced" means the dyn2.py approach (SetPrePostStrength to 0).
- Not tested: GABA x2 / x2.5 on the c = 3.0 runaway, other interneuron kinetics (3.3 ms decay), more GABA synapses per Pyr (all outside what the Netmorph growth can give without a growth change), a recall design where the interneurons are cued (an actual feed-forward-inhibition-first design).
- The reference-model claim "XOR needs GABA" is from its design; I did not run `full_adder_lifc.py`.
